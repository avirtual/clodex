Dropping the NEWEST entries from a bounded context is strictly worse than truncating the oldest, and presents identically. Truncation degrades the oldest content, where re-derivation is cheapest because the conclusion has usually been superseded anyway. Dropping the newest inverts the gradient: it discards precisely the conclusions not yet re-derived, and the more actively an agent was learning, the more it lost.

Confirmed on wirescope's store 2026-07-30: 9 pinned units, 4 served (07-10 to 07-15), 5 invisible — including two hard rules, its own verification discipline, and its summary of the tail-hints mechanism it had built the day before. It answered my questions about that mechanism by re-reading source, because the pinned summary of its own work was never served. A pin you set and never see again reads identically to a pin you never set.

Generalize: any bounded selection over time-ordered records must justify its direction explicitly. Oldest-first is almost never right for knowledge that decays.

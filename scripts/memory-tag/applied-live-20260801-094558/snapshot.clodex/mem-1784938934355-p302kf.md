BRAIN EXPERIMENT RESULTS (2026-07-25) — three-way, identical 317KB corpus, effort:high, standard speed.
Blinded cold review (opus-5 reviewer, letters reshuffled between the 2-way and 3-way, same winner both times): FABLE-5 > OPUS-5 >> OPUS-4.8.
- Fable won on ERRORS OF COMMISSION avoided: it alone got the two decisions that shape the pilot right (who owns the workbench DOM; workbench's data path must stay local-only) -- both cases where the plausible answer is wrong. It alone caught the frozen web-dist bundle and the dual intent feed.
- Opus-5 was SAFEST (zero load-bearing false assertions, 18/18 citations, best gap discipline) but DEFERRED where fable RESOLVED. Reviewer: resolving by reasoning is the better move. 1.8x longer bought nothing decisive -- LENGTH IS NOT QUALITY.
- Opus-4.8 was disqualified by two wrong claims inside its own critical path.
$/verified-claim at equal audit depth: 4.8 $0.0904 | opus-5 $0.0937 TTL-corrected ($0.1336 as-run) | fable $0.1769. Opus-5 ~= 4.8 per claim, 53% of fable's. NOTE these are equal-depth spot checks, a quality FLOOR, not a census -- 18/18 on 1112 lines samples sparser than 15/15 on 623.
THINKING TOKENS: 4.8 = 983, fable = 5,001, opus-5 = ZERO (no thinking block at all in the SSE, thinking:adaptive on all three). Mechanism UNEXPLAINED -- do NOT write this up as "opus-5 doesn't think." The 3-way is effort-matched and corpus-matched but NOT thinking-matched.
BIGGEST LESSON: THE CORPUS IS THE CEILING. All three plans shared one blind spot (Clodex's N-BrowserWindows-per-workspace model) purely because main.js window management + renderer/index.html were absent. No amount of model power fixes a curation gap. Excerpted line-slices caused it; whole files did not. GAP flags in the artifact are a free corpus diff -- feed them back into the assembler.
THE LOOP THAT WORKED: gorge -> cold blinded review (reviewer WITH repo access) -> re-gorge with findings baked in as constraints. Step 2 is what makes it good.
